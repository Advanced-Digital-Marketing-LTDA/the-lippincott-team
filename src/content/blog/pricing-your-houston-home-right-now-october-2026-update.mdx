- Price from recent closed sales near your home, not from sales a year or two old.

If you own a home in Cypress or Northwest Houston, you probably want to know what it is worth, whether now is a good time to sell, and who can market it well. This update covers how the price is set, what the latest Houston numbers show, and what to ask an agent.

## What Goes Into Your Listing Price

The October 20 guide, from a Houston brokerage, describes pricing this way:

- **Use a fresh CMA.** Compare your home with current comparable sales, ideally from the past 60 to 90 days.
- **Price to what buyers will pay now.** A list price above what current buyers will pay is the problem, not impatience.
- **Watch for outdated pricing.** A price based on sales 12 to 24 months old is a warning sign, as are nearby comparable sales at lower prices.
- **Mind the search price bracket.** A meaningful change should move the home into a different online search price bracket and line up with current comparable sales.
- **Rule out other causes.** Poor photos, condition issues, limited marketing exposure and difficult showing access can look like a pricing problem.

## Is Now the Right Time to List?

This is our reading of the numbers above. Prices are flat, homes take a little longer to sell, and buyers have more listings to choose from. With about 30% of active listings already cut, a price set above current sales is easy for buyers to pass over. Your best timing depends on your own plans, but accurate pricing matters more when buyers can compare more homes.

## What to Check After You List

The guide suggests a simple schedule for judging whether the price is working:

- **Days 1 to 7:** evaluate showing traffic.
- **Days 8 to 14:** review buyer and agent feedback.
- **Days 14 to 21:** if showings are not producing offers, pull current comparables.
- **Day 21 and later:** a pricing review is due if activity stays low.

If a cut is needed, the guide says to make it meaningful. Its example is that a $5,000 cut on a $450,000 home rarely changes buyer behavior. It suggests cutting 3 to 5% when you are 3 to 5% above market, 5 to 8% when 5 to 10% above, and 10% or more, possibly in steps, when 10% or more above. It says concessions such as closing-cost help work best as a closing tool, not a substitute for competitive pricing.

## How to Choose an Agent Who Can Price and Market Your Home

Ask these questions in your listing meetings:

- Which recent sales did you use, and how recent are they?
- How does my price compare with the homes buyers will see next to mine?
- How will you track showings and feedback in the first three weeks?
- What is the marketing plan to get strong exposure from day one?
- What would make you recommend a price change, and how big?

### How do I know if my price is too high?

The same guide lists few showings, showings without offers, nearby comparable sales at lower prices, and a price based on sales 12 to 24 months old as signs that price is the problem.

### How long do homes take to sell in Greater Houston?

In September 2026, single-family homes averaged 58 days on market, two days longer than a year earlier, according to <a href="https://myneighborhoodnews.com/houston-housing-market-slows-as-mortgage-rates-rise-but-home-prices-hold-steady-in-september-2026" target="_blank" rel="noopener">HAR data</a>.

### How much should I cut the price if my home is not selling?

The guide suggests 3 to 5%, 5 to 8%, or 10% or more, depending on how far above market you are, as described above. It says a token cut rarely changes buyer behavior.
