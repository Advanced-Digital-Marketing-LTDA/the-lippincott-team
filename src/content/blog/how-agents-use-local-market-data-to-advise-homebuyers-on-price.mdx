Agents advise buyers on price by comparing the home you want with recent nearby sales, then checking how fast homes are selling and how much inventory is available. That tells you whether to offer near the asking price or negotiate. In Greater Houston in September 2026, the median single-family price was $325,000 and supply stood at 5.5 months, according to the Houston Association of Realtors (HAR) September report, [as reported by My Neighborhood News](https://myneighborhoodnews.com/hwy6-cypress/houston-housing-market-slows-as-mortgage-rates-rise-but-home-prices-hold-steady-in-september-2026). Inventory is up from a year ago, so buyers have more homes to compare.

## Key Takeaways

- Agents price a home from recent nearby sales, months of inventory and days on market, not from the asking price alone.
- Greater Houston's median single-family price was $325,000 in September 2026, down 0.3% from a year earlier.
- At [Freddie Mac's](https://www.freddiemac.com/pmms) 7.24% national average (Oct 29, 2026), a $260,000 loan costs about $1,772 a month in principal and interest.
- Ask a lender for a written payment estimate that includes taxes and insurance before choosing a price range.

## What agents look at

**Recent comparable sales.** Agents look at similar homes that closed nearby, not just asking prices. This anchors what a fair price looks like for the street and size of home you want.

**Months of inventory.** This shows how long it would take to sell every active listing at the current pace. More months generally means more choice for buyers.

**Days on market.** Homes that sit longer give you more room to negotiate. Homes that sell fast usually need a quicker decision.

**Active listings and pending sales.** Rising listings and falling pending sales tell your agent that competition is easing.

## What it means for your offer

In September 2026 the median price was flat (down 0.3%) while active listings rose 6.3% and homes took two days longer to sell. That gives a first-time buyer more homes to compare and less pressure to rush. Your agent should still check the specific neighborhood and price range, because a Greater Houston figure can hide big differences.

## Mortgage options to compare

The right loan depends on your down payment, credit and eligibility. These are the common options for Houston-area buyers, based on lender guidance from [Houston Prime Realty](https://houstonprimerealty.com/mortgage-information/) and [LendingTree](https://www.lendingtree.com/home/fha/fha-loan-limits-in-texas/).

| Loan type | Minimum down payment | Key points |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Conventional | 3% to 5% | Credit score generally 620 or higher. Putting 20% down avoids private mortgage insurance. 2026 conforming limit: $832,750. |
| FHA | 3.5% | Needs a 580 or higher score for the 3.5% option. Includes upfront and monthly mortgage insurance. 2026 Harris County limit: $541,287. |
| VA | 0% | For eligible service members and veterans. No monthly mortgage insurance. One-time funding fee applies for most borrowers. |
| USDA | 0% | For eligible rural and outer-suburban areas. Household income limits apply. |

Limits, fees and eligibility change, so confirm the current terms with a lender.

## What you can afford: a worked example

Freddie Mac's national average 30-year fixed rate was 7.24% as of October 29, 2026 (15-year: 6.60%). Using that rate on the $325,000 Houston median price:

| Down payment | Loan amount | Principal and interest per month |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 20% ($65,000) | $260,000 | about $1,772 |
| 5% ($16,250) | $308,750 | about $2,104 |

This is our own calculation for a 30-year fixed loan at 7.24%. It excludes property taxes, homeowners insurance, mortgage insurance and HOA dues. The rate is a national average from [Freddie Mac](https://www.freddiemac.com/pmms), not a quote for you.

## Property tax and insurance

Taxes and insurance sit on top of principal and interest, so include them when you set a price range.

**Property tax.** Harris County's combined rate for its four taxing entities rose from $0.6241 to $0.6715 per $100 of appraised value, adopted September 17, 2026, according to [Ownwell](https://www.ownwell.com/blog/harris-county-property-tax-increase-2026). School, city and any municipal utility district (MUD) taxes are billed on top of that county rate. The 2026 school homestead exemption is $140,000, with an added $60,000 for homeowners 65 and older or disabled, so file a homestead exemption once you own the home.

**Homeowners insurance.** Houston's average homeowners premium was $5,653 a year in 2025 (about $471 a month), according to Insurify data cited by [Live Insurance News](https://www.liveinsurancenews.com/two-bills-hit-houston-homeowners/8574730/) in August 2026. Your quote depends on the home's age, roof, location and coverage.

Because taxes, insurance and any mortgage insurance add to the payment, ask a lender for a written estimate of your full monthly payment before you pick a price range.

## Steps before you make an offer

1. Decide the monthly payment you are comfortable with.
2. Get pre-approved and compare loan options with a lender.
3. Ask your agent for recent sales in your target neighborhoods.
4. Tour homes, then review comparable sales and days on market for each.
5. Agree on an offer price and terms with your agent.

## FAQs

### Do local numbers matter more than national headlines?

Local figures describe the market you will buy in. HAR reported Greater Houston inventory of 5.5 months in September 2026, and your agent adds recent nearby sales for the street and home type you want.

### Does a 5.5-month supply mean I can offer low?

Not automatically. 5.5 months is a Greater Houston figure. Your agent checks your neighborhood and price range before suggesting an offer.

### How do I know what I can afford?

Start with the monthly payment you are comfortable with, then work back to a price. At 7.24%, a $260,000 loan is about $1,772 a month before taxes, insurance and mortgage insurance. A lender can give you a written estimate that includes those costs.

### Are the mortgage rates here my rate?

No. They are Freddie Mac's national weekly averages as of October 29, 2026. Your quote depends on your lender and your finances.

### Which loan is best for a first-time buyer?

It depends on your down payment, credit and eligibility. Conventional and FHA loans need as little as 3% to 3.5% down, while VA and USDA loans can need nothing down if you qualify. A lender can compare them using your numbers.
